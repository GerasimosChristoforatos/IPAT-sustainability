# Dematerialism as a fourth lever: a quantitative check

Reproducible companion to the Perspective. All numbers below are regenerated by `make all`;
inputs and page references are in `data/sources.yaml`, choices in `config.yaml`.

## 1. Question

Can technology (T) alone keep CO₂ within a planetary allowance, and if not, how much
"dematerialism" (Aᵈᵉᵐ) would be required, and has that much been observed anywhere? We
quantify; we do not test causation.

## 2. Equation

```
I = P × ( Aᵉˢˢ + Aᵐᵃᵗ / Aᵈᵉᵐ ) × T          constraint: I ≤ Iᵖˡ
```

Aᵉˢˢ is a sufficiency floor, Aᵐᵃᵗ = A − Aᵉˢˢ, and Aᵈᵉᵐ divides Aᵐᵃᵗ only. As Aᵈᵉᵐ → ∞,
I → P × Aᵉˢˢ × T, the cost of existing. Required dematerialism is
Aᵈᵉᵐ* = Aᵐᵃᵗ / (Iᵖˡ/(P·T) − Aᵉˢˢ). Aᵉˢˢ, Aᵈᵉᵐ and Iᵖˡ are stretched concepts and are
labelled as such in every results table.

## 4. Method

- **Scenarios are counterfactuals, not forecasts:** the 2022 world population (or the peak
  population) lives at the US, EU27 or world GDP per capita, with world T.
- **T scenarios:** today; 2050 endpoints after declines of 1.5 % (BAU), 6.9 % (Ambitious) and
  11.5 % (Extreme) per year; and the best current national T (Norway 0.072, with Sweden
  0.135 alongside because Norway's GDP is inflated by petroleum exports).
- **Iᵖˡ** = budget ÷ 50 years (25 and 100 as sensitivity).
- **Floor:** each route is expressed as φ = floor ÷ current world mean in its own unit, then
  Aᵉˢˢ = φ × world GDP per capita.
- **Frontier:** quantile regression of the ladder on log A at τ = 0.9, solved exactly by
  linear programming, with a 2,000-replication pairs bootstrap. A free-disposal-hull peer
  names the actual country.
- **Uncertainty:** one Monte Carlo (10,000 draws, fixed seed) over Aᵉˢˢ, budget likelihood
  (33–67 %) and horizon (25–100 years), run conditional on each T scenario.
- **Validation:** P × A × T reproduces observed consumption CO₂ for US, EU27 and World.
  Because T is defined as I/(P·A) this is an identity; it checks units and aggregation.
  20 tests pass.

## 5. Results

### RQ1 Burn rate
At today's T, a world living the US lifestyle would emit 132 Gt/yr: 13 times Iᵖˡ for
1.7 °C, exhausting that budget in 3.8 years. The EU lifestyle gives 8.8 times (5.7 years);
the actual world 3.8 times (13 years). At the BAU endpoint the US lifestyle is still 8.6
times over. At the Ambitious endpoint it is 1.8 times over for 1.7 °C and **within** the
allowance for 2.0 °C (0.85). At the Extreme endpoint it is within for 1.7 and 2.0 °C.
Nothing but the world lifestyle under Extreme fits 1.5 °C.

The path-consistent version (everyone at the reference A from now, T declining from today)
is harsher for rich lifestyles: at 6.9 %/yr the US lifestyle exhausts the 1.7 °C budget in
2032 and the 2.0 °C budget in 2046. It is kinder to the world lifestyle, whose cumulative
emissions at 6.9 %/yr are bounded at 394 Gt and never exhaust the 1.7 °C budget. At peak
population all ratios rise by 28 %.

### RQ2 The floor
Six routes give φ between 0.28 and 0.54. **Aᵉˢˢ = $6,350 per person per year (range
$4,470–8,710)**, or 1.8 t CO₂ per person at today's T (Fig. 2). The routes rest on about
four independent lineages: Hickel & Sullivan synthesise the energy and materials models
used by two other routes. The highest route (Vélez-Henao, recent technology) says decent
living with current provisioning needs 54 % of current emissions.

### RQ3 Price of materialism
**At least 69 % of global CO₂, about 26 Gt/yr, is attributable to consumption above the
floor** (62–76 % across the floor range). This is a lower bound from group means (Jensen's
inequality); the best-fitting Lorenz curve (Singh–Maddala) gives the same 69 %, so the bound
is tight. The top 10 % account for about 16.5 Gt of it. About 3 Gt/yr of floor is currently unmet
by the bottom half (Fig. 3).

### RQ4 Cost of existing
P × Aᵉˢˢ × T is 14.7 Gt/yr at today's T: 1.5 times Iᵖˡ for 1.7 °C and 5.5 times for 1.5 °C.
**The floor alone does not fit today.** At the BAU endpoint it just fits 1.7 °C (0.96, and
fails at the high floor or at peak population). It fits 1.5 °C only under Ambitious or
Extreme T. Equal shares at today's T for 1.7 °C mean $4,320 per person, roughly Ghana's GDP
per capita; at the BAU endpoint $6,600 (Bolivia); at the Ambitious endpoint $32,000
(Poland). For orientation only, the World Bank's upper-middle-income poverty line is
$8.30 per day (2021 PPP, household consumption); it is not directly comparable with GDP in
2011 prices.

### RQ5 Required dematerialism
For the US lifestyle, 50-year horizon, 2022 population:

| T scenario | 1.5 °C | 1.7 °C | 2.0 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | floor fails | floor fails | 18.7 |
| BAU | floor fails | 204 | 6.8 |
| Sweden's T | floor fails | 17.8 | 3.9 |
| Norway's T | floor fails | 4.6 | 1.7 |
| Ambitious | 22.5 | **2.0** | none needed |
| Extreme | 1.7 | none needed | none needed |

EU27 values are about 40 % lower (1.2 at Ambitious, 1.7 °C).

### RQ6 Is Aᵈᵉᵐ real?
The τ = 0.9 frontier has a slope of 0.71 ladder points per log unit (95 % interval
0.47–0.86; published crude cross-country slope about 0.6). The US life evaluation (6.72) is
reached on the frontier at $21,200 per person, against $57,100 in the US:
**observed Aᵈᵉᵐ = 3.4 (2.4–5.7)**. For EU27 it is 2.9 (2.0–5.3). The named peers are Costa
Rica for the US (ratio 5.4) and Mexico for EU27 (3.4). With τ = 0.95 the US estimate rises
to 6.3; with the 2023–2025 ladder it is 3.7. A quadratic term is not significant (p = 0.53),
so we find no satiation within the sample (Fig. 4).

**Required versus observed (Fig. 1).** Observed Aᵈᵉᵐ exceeds what is required only where T
has already done most of the work: at 1.7 °C with Ambitious T (required 2.0), at 2.0 °C
with Norway's T (1.7). With BAU T it falls short even for 2.0 °C (6.8 required) and with
Sweden's T it is marginal (3.9). The Monte Carlo agrees: under Ambitious T at 1.7 °C the
median required Aᵈᵉᵐ is 2.3 (90 % interval 1.2–4.7) for the US and observed Aᵈᵉᵐ suffices in
82 % of draws; under BAU T it suffices in 0 % (1.7 °C) and 5 % (2.0 °C); for 1.5 °C under
Ambitious T the median is 41 and the floor fails in 34 % of draws.

## 6. Limitations

- **GDP is a proxy for consumption, not for wanting.** The frontier shows that some
  countries report equal life evaluation at a third of the GDP; it does not show they want
  less, nor that a rich country could move there. Income, not green identity, predicts
  footprints (Moser & Kleinhückelkotten 2018), and efficiency gains rebound (Brockway et al.
  2021).
- **Cross-section, not cause.** Frontier countries differ in climate, institutions and
  inequality. The bootstrap covers country sampling, not survey error in the ladder.
- **Floor conversion** assumes physical requirements scale with GDP at today's technology.
- **Iᵖˡ** is a flat share of a cumulative budget; results scale directly with the horizon.
  Budget likelihoods cover TCRE uncertainty only.
- **Fossil CO₂ only.** Land-use change (4.3 Gt in 2022) counts against the budget but is not
  in I, so burn rates are understated.
- **Year gaps.** Activity data are for 2022, the budget starts in 2026 (fossil CO₂ was 4 %
  higher in 2025), and T endpoints are for 2050 while the population peak is in 2084.
- **T rates** come from a production-based energy-CO₂ metric and were not read in a primary
  PwC document (the supplied file contains only the 2023 rate and the 1.5 °C rate). In our
  own data, consumption-based world T fell 1.76 %/yr over 2000–2022.
- **Norway** is the best national T under any rule; its GDP flatters its T.

## 7. What the numbers do and do not support

They support three parts of the thesis. At the only decarbonisation rate sustained so far,
T does not bring any reference lifestyle, or even the floor at 1.5 °C, within the allowance.
Equal shares at today's T land at the GDP level of Ghana. And T and Aᵈᵉᵐ behave as
complements: without faster T the floor itself fails and no Aᵈᵉᵐ can help, while with
Ambitious T a dematerialism of about 2, less than the 3.4 we estimate is already observed,
closes the gap at 1.7 °C.

They limit the thesis in three ways. First, claim 1 holds for achieved rates, not for all
rates: a sustained 6.9 %/yr brings the US lifestyle within the 2.0 °C allowance with no
dematerialism at all, and equal shares then sit at Poland's level, not at poverty. Second,
Aᵈᵉᵐ is decisive only in a narrow band, around 1.7 °C with Ambitious T; at 1.5 °C the
requirement is far beyond anything observed, and under BAU T it is beyond it even at
2.0 °C. Third, "observed" means observed as a cross-country pattern in GDP and reported
life evaluation. We estimate that the combination exists; we do not show that it is a
capacity to want less, or that it can be produced.
